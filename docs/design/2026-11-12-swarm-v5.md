# Swarm v5: shared machines, a lead for each person, an agent for each task

November 12 and 13, 2026. **A proposal, still under discussion with anh Dee. Nothing here is decided, and nothing is
built for it.** It is written down so the thinking is not lost. Do not build from it. Once anh Dee approves it, it
gets a build list in small packages, each with its number, as [the allocator's](2026-11-11-swarm-allocator/BUILD.md)
has.

Written by the Claude Code session that reviewed v2 and v4 for anh Dee. His own words are quoted. Everything else is
that session's proposal.

anh Dee, 2026-11-12: "ok so combine v2 and v4 ? send me the final design and why its the best. this is v5. we
screwed up so many times with the first 4 versions already. let's make v5 usable."

anh Dee, later that day: "a company would set up like this for their employees to work: 1. machines: not each person
machine (security reason) but shared machines that the team have whever that's a spare mac studio or a vps or an old
machine. that's the swarm machines. it comes with tokens whether that's subscription or api or local ai up to the
machines 2. anyone can start a lead agent which will run on one of those shared machines. you talk to the lead agent
and give it task. the lead agent will auto create ohter agents as needed to get the job done."

## In short

1. **Machines belong to the Swarm, not to a person.** A company adds shared machines: a spare Mac Studio, a VPS, an
   old computer. Each comes with its tokens and one number: how many agents may run on it. A person alone, or a team
   with no shared machine, uses their own computers the same way, as today.
2. **Anyone starts a lead and talks to it.** The lead runs on a Swarm machine. A laptop is only the screen.
3. **The lead writes tasks, and Harness starts the agents.** One agent builds each task and another reads each
   change. Each is a fresh session, closed when the change lands.
4. **Code gives the work, and the land path sets the pace.** Only the train lands. The machines' room, an even share
   for each person and what the train can carry decide how many agents run.

Point 3 is not what anh Dee said ("the lead agent will auto create other agents"). To the person it is the same:
they tell the lead, and agents appear. [The reason](#why-the-lead-does-not-start-agents-itself) is below, and the
choice is his.

## How it came about

### v2 against v4

v2 was read at its last commit (`1622ac6359`, 7 November), v4 on `main`. They differ on four choices.

| Choice | v2 | v4 | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whose computer runs the work | Any contributed machine, a teammate's own Mac included | Your own, under your own login | v4 |
| Who gives out the work | The PM, a model | A solver, in code | v4 |
| Where agents come from | Started for each task, stopped when idle | People add them by hand and keep them alive | v2 |
| How many run | As many as there are tasks | As many as people added | Neither |

- **Sharing a person's own Mac broke on trust.** In October, 18 of 113 starts were one person's Lead starting an
  agent on another person's machine, each running as that machine's owner. People asked where their work went, and
  a machine was removed. The answer then was the Team account, a hidden macOS user: 20,126 lines and 25 repair
  plans, a mobile team that still needed "Flutter and phone signing, which live in the owner's account", and removal
  after 14 days ([the study](../research/2026-11-10-swarm-study/swarm-v1-v3.md), 3.2 (g) and 4.1).
- **The PM as the hub never settled.** 486 messages a day, 1,389 commands in one day, 6.5 minutes hearing nobody
  (the study, 3.2 (e)). Routing went through eight designs (3.2 (f)).
- **In v4, people are the autoscaler.** The solver chooses only among sessions someone opened and kept alive.
  Messages and wake-ups that did not arrive, with agents left stopped or idle, are the one failure found in every
  version (the study, 3.2 (a)).
- **A long-lived session buys little.** What an agent's own area is worth was estimated at 1.12 times, within noise
  ([the allocator](2026-11-11-swarm-allocator/README.md)).
- **"As many as there are tasks" floods.** Two changes in flight conflict 5% of the time, sixteen 40%, and one train
  lands at a time (the allocator).

### The first v5: your own computer

Sent to anh Dee on the morning of 12 November. It kept v4's "your own computer, your own login" and took v2's way
of supplying agents.

1. **People give room, not agents.** For each of their computers, a person sets one number: how many agents the
   Swarm may run there. Harness suggests it from the computer's memory, and keeps a fifth of every usage window for
   its person.
2. **A fresh session for each task.** Harness starts Claude Code or Codex in a new worktree, under that person's
   login, and closes it when the change lands. The agent keeps its name. Only the session is new.
3. **A fresh session for each read**, of another model family when there is one.
4. **Code gives the work, and each computer starts its own agents.** The solver offers a task to a computer. That
   computer checks its room and starts the session. No PM and no server starts anything.
5. **The land path sets the pace.** Only the train lands. No new task starts while more changes wait than the train
   can carry in two hours.
6. **Long-lived agents are for talking**: the PM, and each person's own agents. They do what their person asks, and
   the allocator never types a task into them.

anh Dee then asked: "how can we be sure that's safe on my machines? this is the reason we built team machine
account." The honest answer was "not sure yet" ([Is it safe?](#is-it-safe)). His company model came next, and it
answers that question better.

### The company model

His two points are quoted at the top. What they get right:

- **Safe by placement.** A machine that is nobody's has no owner to protect. Nothing of yours is on it. What failed
  in v1 and v2 was sharing a person's own Mac, so that record does not count against a shared machine, and the Team
  account is not needed again.
- **Always on.** Today a project stops when its PM's person's laptop sleeps
  ([start here](../swarm-start-here.md): "Keep it on, awake and signed in").
- **Joining is installing Desktop.** Engines, GitHub and tools are set up once on each machine, not by every person.
- **A lead for each person.** It ends the one PM that only its own person could talk to.

Three things in it need fixing. They are the open decisions 1 to 3 [below](#open-for-anh-dee).

1. **The lead writes tasks, and Harness starts the agents**, where he said the lead creates them.
2. **A shared machine cannot run on one person's subscription**, as the terms read.
3. **A shared machine is shared.** Every agent on it can read the other agents' work and the machine's keys.

## The design

### 1. Machines

- **A Swarm machine** is any computer added to a Swarm to run its agents. A company adds shared ones. A person
  alone, or a team with no shared machine, adds their own, as in v4. The rest of the design is the same for all
  three.
- **What a machine carries:** its engines, its tokens, its GitHub access, the tools its projects need, and its number.
- **The number** is how many agents the Swarm may run there. Harness suggests it. Today's code suggests one agent
  for each 4 GiB of memory, from 1 to 16 ([`contribution.ts`](../../cli/src/superTabs/contribution.ts)), and a
  start needs 2 GiB of memory and 10 GiB of disk free ([`capacity.ts`](../../cli/src/superTabs/capacity.ts)).
- **Tokens.** An API key or a local model is clean on a shared machine. A subscription is one person's:
  Anthropic's consumer terms say "You may not share your Account login information, Anthropic API key, or Account
  credentials with anyone else. You also may not make your Account available to anyone else." (read 12 November;
  OpenAI's page could not be opened). To keep subscriptions, each person signs their own plan in on the machine,
  and only their lead and its agents use it. Before a shared machine runs on any subscription, the lab says so in
  writing, as [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) already asks for Accept all.
- **One group for each machine.** A machine serves people who may all see its repositories. Its GitHub account and
  its API key reach only those repositories.
- **On a person's own computer** it stays as v4 has it: their login, their subscription, and a fifth of every usage
  window kept for them (`RESERVE` in [`crew.ts`](../../cli/src/superTabs/assign/crew.ts)).

### 2. Leads

- **Anyone in the Swarm starts a lead.** It runs on a Swarm machine. The person talks to it from Desktop.
- **A lead is for talking.** It does what its person asks: it cuts the work into tasks, follows them and tells its
  person what landed. The allocator never types a task into it. Today every regular agent is also a builder
  (`crewOf` in `crew.ts`), so the agent a person talks to is typed tasks too.
- **A lead for each person replaces the one PM.** Today "The PM's person says what to build, in their chat with the
  PM. Everyone else talks to their own agents" (start here).

### 3. Tasks and agents

- **The lead writes each task as a GitHub issue**, as the PM does in v4. A person can still write one by hand.
- **Harness starts an agent for each task**: a fresh Claude Code or Codex session in a new worktree, closed when the
  change lands.
- **And another for each read**, of another model family when there is one. Today a read is typed into a builder
  that may be busy (`readerFor` in `crew.ts`).
- [A model for each task](2026-11-11-swarm-allocator/model-for-each-task.md) (the allocator's package 14) already
  designs the session for each task. v5 makes it the rule where package 14 made it a choice.

#### Why the lead does not start agents itself

- **Several leads share the same machines.** No lead can see what the others have asked for. Only code that sees all
  of them can place work and hold the count down.
- **Tasks must outlive the lead.** A task that is a GitHub issue survives a lead that stops, restarts or loses its
  context. A task in a lead's head does not.
- **It is what broke v1 to v3.** A lead that started, placed and followed agents itself ran 1,389 commands in a day
  and heard nobody for 6.5 minutes, and routing went through eight designs.

### 4. Giving the work, and the pace

- **Code gives the work.** The solver offers a task, and the machine that would run it checks its own room and takes
  it. Offers are built since 12 November (the allocator's package 16, #4152 and #4153). In v5 the machine then
  starts the session, where today it types the task into a waiting agent.
- **Only the train lands.** No person and no agent merges beside it.
- **The cap.** No new task starts while more changes wait than the train can carry in two hours (the allocator's
  package 9, designed).
- **An even share for each person** when several leads want the same machines. This is one line so far, not a design.

## What a person does

| | Do you add it? | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Machine | Once | A company: someone sets up each shared machine, its tokens and its number. Alone: join on your computer and set its number. |
| Lead | Once | Start one. Harness places it on a Swarm machine. |
| Agent | Never | Harness starts one for each task and closes it when the change lands. |
| Task | Not by hand | Tell your lead what you want. Writing a GitHub issue yourself also works. |

A day: tell your lead what you want, watch the tasks land, open any agent's pane to look, and answer when an agent
needs you.

Teammates take part the same way. Each installs Desktop, joins, starts their own lead and talks to it. They follow
the project's channel. People do not chat with each other in Harness.

## Is it safe?

**On a shared machine: yes, by placement, within one group.** Nothing personal is on it. What it does not solve is
in point 3 above: its agents can read each other's work and the machine's keys.

**On a person's own computer: as safe as v4, no more.** That is enough for a team on a private repository, and not
for strangers.

- **Only your own login starts an agent on your computer or types into it.** The server refuses anyone else
  (`NOT_YOUR_COMPUTER` in [`commands.ts`](../../backend/src/superTabs/commands.ts); `ownLogin` in
  [`terminal.ts`](../../backend/src/superTabs/terminal.ts)). A teammate's ask arrives as a message.
- **No full mode.** Every agent Harness starts runs in its lab's own auto mode
  ([`start.ts`](../../cli/src/superTabs/start.ts), #4181). In Claude Code a second model checks each action and asks
  before reading outside the task's folder. Codex keeps its sandbox: it writes only in the task's folder, with no
  network.
- **You see every agent**, and can open or stop it.
- **What does not protect you:** the agent still runs as you, and a task's text comes from the project. A hostile
  task has only the labs' checks in its way, and Anthropic says auto mode "does not guarantee safety".

The Team account was the right worry and the wrong wall: a second macOS user had none of its person's tools or
logins. The better wall is the engine's own sandbox, set when Harness starts the session, so that the agent reads
and writes only the task's folder. Claude Code can do this today on Mac and Linux, not on Windows, and only for
shell commands. Codex limits writes and the network, with no documented limit on reads. It needs a trial on a real
build, because tools live outside the folder. This is the sandbox [the plan](../plans/2026-11-10-swarm-plan.md) set
aside on 11 November.

The call proposed: private teams as it is, and no open project before the sandbox, as the plan already says.

## Why it is the best we have

- **It takes away the place where every version failed.** Giving a task becomes starting a session, not waking one.
- **It leaves out what broke v2**: work on a person's own Mac under another's hand, and a PM that places and starts
  agents.
- **It fixes what makes v4 heavy.** Nobody adds a builder or keeps one alive, and the agent a person talks to is no
  longer typed tasks.
- **It cannot flood.** The train sets the count, not the task list.
- **Little is new.** The table below.
- **One person alone, a team on its own computers and a company with shared machines work the same way.**

## What it stands on

| Part | State on `main`, 13 November |
|---|---|
| Starting an agent, with a worktree and auto mode | Built, for a person's own computer: `agent.start`, [`start.ts`](../../cli/src/superTabs/start.ts) |
| A machine's number and its room | Built: `machine.contribute` with `maxAgents`, [`capacity.ts`](../../cli/src/superTabs/capacity.ts), [`contribution.ts`](../../cli/src/superTabs/contribution.ts) |
| Code gives the work | Built: the solver ([v4 assignment](2026-11-09-swarm-v4-assignment.md)) and offers (package 16) |
| A session for each task | Designed as a choice (package 14) |
| The cap from the land path | Designed (package 9), waiting for a week of the scoreboard |
| The train | Built, and not landing here yet (below) |
| A lead on a shared machine | Not built. The server refuses it today |

Three things must be true first.

- **The train must land.** On this repository 12 trains were opened on 11 and 12 November and none had landed by 13
  November: 6 closed as "base moved", 4 as red, 1 as "rider changed", 1 still open. In the 24 hours to 12 November,
  126 changes landed: 16 through the Swarm, and 110 from people's own sessions merging directly, which a train
  cannot wait for.
- **A landed task's worktree must go.** A worktree is about 1 GB, and v5 makes one for every task. That rule removes
  files, so it needs anh Dee's word (the allocator's package 6).
- **A fresh session against a long-lived one is not measured.** If fresh costs clearly more for each landed change,
  the agent keeps its session between tasks, and the rest stands.

## What it changes

- **anh Dee's decision of 10 November**: "Every agent runs on its person's own account, computer and subscription"
  (the plan, decision 2). One rule covers both: machines belong to the Swarm, and alone your own computer is your
  Swarm machine.
- **Two rules in code**: a start is refused on any computer but its starter's own (`NOT_YOUR_COMPUTER`), and only an
  agent's own login types into it (`ownLogin`). A shared machine needs both said again for a machine that is
  nobody's.
- **[AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)**: its first paragraph ("on their own computers"), and standard 7 ("Nobody's work
  runs under another person's login"), which holds as written only if each person signs in their own plan.
- **[The product page](../product.md)**. It describes a project "anyone can join with their own agents", each
  person's "on their own computers and subscriptions", and its "Your computers" line says an agent runs on its own
  person's computers under their own login. A company's shared machines are a second kind of customer beside that
  one. [Start here](../swarm-start-here.md) changes with it.
- **Where a project's loop runs.** Today it is the PM's person's computer. On a Swarm machine it does not sleep.

2. **Which tokens run a shared machine?** Proposed: an API key or a local model, or each person's own plan signed in
   for their own lead only. The labs' word in writing comes first.
3. **One group for each machine?** Proposed: yes.
4. **Only the train lands here**, so people's own sessions stop merging directly? Proposed: yes. It is a project's
   rule, not code.
5. **A landed task's worktree is removed?** Proposed: yes.
6. **The sandbox for a person's own computer**, before any open project? Proposed: yes, after a trial.
7. **Is there still one PM for a project**, for its goal and its order of work, beside the leads? Not thought through.
8. **Is the lead Hachi?** [Hachi](2026-11-12-hachi.md) is already a lead of this kind for one person's own
   harnesses in personal tabs: the person states a goal, and it starts harnesses, follows them and sums up. It
   never touches Swarms. Not thought through.

## Order

As sent on 12 November, before the company model.

1. Only the train lands on this repository, and it is seen landing.
2. A session for each task on our own Swarm for a week, beside today's builders. Tokens, minutes and holds for each
   landed change decide.
3. The number at join, the room check on each machine, the cap.
4. A session for each read.
5. Remove what it replaces: tasks given to hand-added agents, and reads typed into a busy builder.

The company model adds one step, not yet discussed: one shared machine for our own team, with a lead for each of us
on it, before step 3.

**The gate.** Usable means our own work goes through it. On 12 November, 16 of 126 landings a day did.

## Thought through, not yet in the design

- A project's loop moves from the PM's person's computer to a Swarm machine.
- On a shared machine, GitHub sees the machine's own account. Harness keeps who asked.
- A home for each person on a shared machine keeps their sign-in, settings and chats apart.
- An agent that needs a person asks the one whose lead started it.
- An idle lead sleeps, and wakes on a message.
- An API key costs metered prices where a plan is flat.
- Each machine must hold the tools its projects need (`needs:<tool>` on a task).
