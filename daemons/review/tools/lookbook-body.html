NAME

daemons — pair-programming buddies that hatch from your work and live in your status line

One daemon pairs with you at a time. It watches every harness on every machine, tells you what needs you, and runs the orchestration you ask for. Every daemon can do the whole job; they differ in lore, look and voice. Each is named after a piece of terminal history, the way tim is tmux improved.

Proposal, 26 Sep 2026; drop 1, init, released 27 Sep 2026. Names, odds, art and lines are drafts. Section 6 of the Unix manual is for games.

SYNOPSIS

hn zoo                   # your daemons and eggs
hn hatch  [egg]          # open an egg when you have a moment
hn pair   [daemon]       # choose who pairs with you
hn logbook [daemon]      # your story together

Commands are proposals. The desktop app does the same things with a click on the status line.

DESCRIPTION

The buddy sits at the right end of the status line, in eight cells. Its eyes carry the mood; a part of its body turns while agents work. Its panel shows its portrait, in the same mood. When it speaks, the status line becomes tmux's yellow message line for a moment. Try the events below, or click the buddy.

DROP 1: INIT

    

      

        The first drop: ten animals hiding in Unix names, drawn filled and in
        colour.
      

      

        The drop is called init: init(8) is PID 1, the first
        process a Unix machine starts, and the parent of every daemon (when a
        daemon's parent dies, init adopts it). These daemons are shaded the way
        line printers shaded the Mona Lisa, with denser characters for more
        light. Each one is a model made of shapes, not a hand drawing, so every
        size, mood, growth stage and animation frame is printed from the same
        model. The shiny look is the one hatch in
        256 that comes out gold.

        The names, lore, colours and art are drafts. In the status line every
        drop keeps eight cells of line art in the bar's own colour (a filled
        daemon cannot be eight characters wide); the filled plates show in
        portraits, cards, the zoo and the reveal. tim keeps his job from before:
        common, and the likeliest first hatch, so everyone meets him. A terminal
        prints the colour the same way: one xterm-256 colour per row from each
        daemon's two stops, dimmer for faint characters.

Hatching individuals

    

      

        An egg waits until you open it. Each hatch creates an individual with
        its own seed, name, bond, and identity. Another tim is another octopus
        on your shelf; it never consumes the one you already know.
      

      

        The first four hatches are new species whenever the egg can give one.
        Later, eight hatches without a new species make the next eligible hatch
        a new one. Secrets come only from eligible eggs and sit outside the nine
        regular species. The server owns the draw; this page lets you inspect
        the art of a chosen outcome.
      

      

        The earlier duplicate-to-XP simulator has been retired.
        Read the individual design.
      

    

  


  

    
EGGS

    

      

        What you do picks the egg; luck picks what is inside. Longer efforts
        earn eggs with rarer pools, the way longer walks do in Pokémon Go. Eggs
        wait in the nest until you open them; when it is full, new ones wait
        their turn. A turn counts when it finishes in a harness you work in
        (never a replay, a sub-agent or a plain terminal), and at most 20 count
        a day across all your machines, so nobody burns tokens farming eggs.

        eggs, and they come back every year so nobody misses one for good.
      

    

  


  

    
GROWTH

    

      

        A daemon keeps its name and face for life. It grows by releases: 0.1
        when it hatches, 1.0 at bond level 2, 2.0 at bond level 4. Bond comes
        from pairing: every turn you finish together, and the first turn of each
        day. Each individual grows through its own bond with you. It never
        decays. The versions grow as NetHack's pets do, the same animal
        throughout: the hatchling is mostly head.

MOODS

    

      

        The eyes are the face, as in cowsay and Japanese kaomoji. The body stays
        still, and moods change at most two cells. Moods come from work, never
        from the clock. Every gesture is finite and ends at rest.
      

      

        

          
          
        

      

      

      

      

        Blinks mean something. A daemon never blinks on a timer or while you
        type:
      

      
    
        
  • 
          I saw that. One blink when something it watches changes: a
          harness needs you, a turn finishes, a test fails.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          I see you. One blink when you look at it: hover it, open its
          panel, or come back to the window.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          I trust you. A slow blink, the way cats show trust: when you
          return after a break, when you meet, when it levels up.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Concentrating. No blinks while agents work, the way people
          blink less when they focus. Eyes stay on =.

MEMORY

    

      

        Work memory belongs to you; stories belong to each daemon. Swapping
        buddies loses nothing about your work, and each daemon keeps its own
        history with you.

LEARNING

    

      

        Hermes gets better at Hermes. A daemon gets better at your whole team of
        agents.
      

      

        Hermes Agent
        (Nous Research) improves itself through files, not training. After a
        reply, a background copy of the agent rereads the conversation and saves
        reusable skills as SKILL.md folders, plus short notes about you. A
        weekly curator archives skills nobody uses. Everything it learns stays
        in ~/.hermes, so Claude Code, Codex and the rest never see
        it. Its prompt tells it to “be active”, and Hermes' own issue tracker
        lists saving the wrong things as a high-priority bug.
      

      

        Harness already sees every turn from every agent on every machine. That
        makes the daemon the one place where a lesson learned in Codex can reach
        Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot and Hermes. The loop:
      

      

        

          
            

              step
              what happens
            

          
          
            

              notice
              
                Only real signals count: you corrected an agent, two agents
                failed the same way, the same steps were repeated, or a skill
                loaded and then needed fixing. The default is that there is
                nothing to learn.
              
            

            

              propose
              
                The daemon says it in one line, in its own voice, and waits for
                your answer. Nothing is taught without your yes.
              
            

            

              teach
              
                The lesson becomes a SKILL.md, the one format all five agents
                load, or a note in the project's AGENTS.md. The
                Agent Skills folders reach
                Claude Code, Codex, Copilot, Cursor and Hermes.
              
            

            

              borrow
              
                What each agent learns on its own (Hermes skills, Claude's
                memory, Codex memories) becomes a candidate for the others.
                Harness reads those stores and never writes into them.
              
            

            

              check
              
                Every lesson records where it came from and whether it gets
                used. Unused lessons are archived after 30 days and put away
                after 90. Any lesson can be reverted.

A daemon learns three kinds of things:

      
    
        
  • 
          About you: how you like to work. This goes in the notebook.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          About your projects: commands, conventions and traps. These
          become skills and AGENTS.md notes.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          About your agents: which one is good at what, and where each
          one stumbles. Only Harness can see this, because only Harness watches
          them all. “codex has passed the billing tests 3 times out of 3, claude
          once. give this one to codex?”
        
    • 
      

      

        In the game, every lesson you approve is a trick the daemon learned.
        Lessons live in the shared notebook, so swapping daemons keeps them all,
        and the daemon that found a lesson gets the credit in its logbook.
        Approved lessons also grow bond.
      

      
    
        
  • 
          Agent output is treated as untrusted. A 2026 study of 21
          self-improving agent setups found that every one of them saved unsafe
          lessons, so every lesson is scanned for injected instructions and
          scripts are never switched on automatically.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Secrets, paths and usernames are removed. Lessons live outside your
          repos unless you ask for them there.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Lessons are distilled in batches while you are idle, with a small
          model, not on every turn.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Every approval is one commit in a git-backed folder, so every lesson
          can be reverted.
        
    • 
      

    

  


  

    
NAMES

    

      
Decided 26 Sep 2026.

      

        

          
            

              thing
              name
              why
            

          
          
            

              the creatures
              daemons
              
                Unix's always-on helpers, named at MIT's Project MAC (1963)
                after Maxwell's demon. Unix has drawn its daemons as little
                creatures for decades. In Philip Pullman's novels a dæmon is an
                animal companion bonded to one person. All three fit.
              
            

            

              the collection
              zoo
              
                Plain English for a place full of creatures, and a real archive
                format from the BBS days (1986) that held collections of files.
                hn zoo.
              
            

            

              hatching
              hatch
              
                The verb stays plain. The reveal says “fork() returned 0.”,
                because every Unix process is born from fork().
              
            

            

              the background service
              harnessd
              
                So “daemon” can mean the creature, user-facing text calls the
                service harnessd, the way sshd and httpd are named. Code keeps
                its names.
              
            

          
        

      

      

        Passed over: /usr/games (long to type), ~/bin (reads as trash), cronies
        (a pun that wears thin), procs (collides with harness ps).
      

    

  


  

    
CAVEATS

    

      
    
        
  • Everything is earned by work you would do anyway. No chores.
    • 
        
  • 
          Only turns you started count, with a daily cap, so nobody spends
          tokens farming eggs.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Eggs never hatch on their own. Nothing moves while you type or while
          an agent is printing.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          No hunger, decay, death, guilt or streaks to lose. A daemon resting in
          the zoo keeps its bond.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Bond never grants permissions. What a buddy may do alone is your
          setting.
        
    • 
        
  • No money: no paid eggs, no loot boxes.
    • 
        
  • 
          Draws happen on the server, so rarity is real and cards can be shared.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Printable 7-bit ASCII only. It reads the same in every terminal, font
          and phone, and pastes into Slack and GitHub inside a code block.
        
    •
